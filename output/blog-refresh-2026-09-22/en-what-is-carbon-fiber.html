What Is Carbon Fiber? A Part Selection Guide for MOE Projects

A useful carbon fiber enquiry to MOE Composite starts with the job of the part. Where will it fit, what will it carry, and which surfaces will remain visible? A machine cover selected for its appearance and a panel transferring loads through its fasteners need different briefs. This guide explains how to turn a catalogue image into a clear manufacturing enquiry.

The fiber is only one part of the material

Carbon fiber is a reinforcement. In a finished composite component, it works with a resin system. Fiber arrangement, resin and manufacturing process all matter, so “carbon” alone is not a complete specification. A visible woven pattern does not establish the load capacity of the finished part.

Mark four areas on the drawing

  • Mating surfaces: Show where the part sits and identify the references used for measurement.
  • Fasteners: Locate hole centres, edge distances and the proposed fixing hardware.
  • Functional regions: Identify expected loads, contact areas and moving interfaces.
  • Visible surfaces: Provide a reference for weave direction, gloss and acceptable surface marks.

If a production drawing is unavailable, send a dimensioned sketch with photographs of the installation. List unknown dimensions explicitly. A photograph can explain the arrangement, but it cannot establish a tolerance or a service load.

A worked enquiry: a visible machine cover

This is an illustrative purchasing scenario, not a completed customer project. For a cover fixed at four corners, define the overall dimensions and hole positions first. Explain whether the cover simply encloses the machine or supports additional equipment. Record the cosmetic finish separately. This gives the supplier distinct functional and visual requirements to review.

Does the application require carbon fiber?

Assess the material against the target weight, operating environment, appearance, quantity and budget. State whether you need actual carbon reinforcement or are describing a visual effect. Asking for a comparison with a glass-fiber option can help clarify the requirement. The MOE product catalogue offers application examples to support that discussion.

Agree on sample acceptance before delivery

Define measurement locations, the installation trial and the viewing conditions for surface inspection before the sample arrives. Visual approval alone does not confirm that fasteners line up or that the part meets its intended function. Keep the approved revision identifiable and record any changes requested afterwards.

For an international enquiry, also state the drawing units and the delivery destination. Distinguish the required sample date from the desired production delivery date; they represent different stages of the project.

Send MOE Composite a project enquiry with the drawing or sketch, expected quantity, service conditions and sample acceptance requirements. Read the composite quality control guide when preparing the inspection portion of the brief.
