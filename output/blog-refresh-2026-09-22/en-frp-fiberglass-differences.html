FRP, GRP and Fiberglass: Writing a Clear MOE Enquiry

An enquiry for a “fiberglass body” and a quotation for a “GRP shell” may describe the same component. They may also hide differences in resin, finish or delivery scope. For MOE Composite projects involving ride cabins, decorative figures and custom bodies, the useful comparison begins with the part specification rather than the material label.

Understand the terminology

FRP means fiber-reinforced polymer and is an umbrella term. GFRP and GRP identify glass-fiber composites. Fiberglass refers to glass reinforcement, although commercial enquiries also use it for finished components. In Turkish supplier documents, CTP commonly means glass-fiber reinforced polyester. A request labelled “fiberglass” does not, on its own, specify the resin system.

Separate the shape from the function

A photograph of a ride cabin shows its theme, colour and external form. It does not define the interface with the supporting structure, the fixing points or the access needed for maintenance. When referencing the MOE amusement ride catalogue, mark the features you want to retain and those you need to change.

Supply of a composite outer body is a different scope from designing and assessing a complete amusement ride. Identify who is responsible for the mechanical assembly, support structure, installation and required documentation. Record those boundaries in the enquiry so that a shell quotation is not mistaken for a complete equipment offer.

Compare quotations against the same checklist

  • Are the dimensions, quantities and supplied components equivalent?
  • Are reinforcement and resin identified beyond the commercial material name?
  • Do colour, gloss and visible-surface references match?
  • Which internal and external surfaces receive the specified finish?
  • Are holes, inserts, trimming and assembly included?
  • Are tooling, initial samples, packing and freight listed separately?

Describe outdoor service conditions

Go beyond “outdoor use”. Explain exposure to sunlight, cleaning practices, expected contact and storage conditions. If the part encounters chemicals, identify the substance and the conditions of use. A photograph of a glossy finish cannot establish suitability for those conditions.

Make an international enquiry easy to evaluate

Start with a product reference, dimensional changes and an interface sketch. Add the operating environment, surface requirements and quantity. Use consistent units across the drawing and written brief. For export orders, specify the delivery destination and which party will arrange transport, rather than assuming that every quotation includes the same logistics scope.

Finally, define what the first sample must demonstrate. A mounting trial and an agreed set of measurements create a clearer decision than approval based only on appearance.
